Why do some people achieve extraordinary things... while others, with similar abilities, never reach their potential?

In The Magic of Thinking Big, David Schwartz argues that success isn't shaped only by intelligence or talent. It's also shaped by the size of our beliefs, and the size of our ambitions.

When we think small, we ask ourselves: What if I fail? What if I'm not good enough? Questions like these make us reject opportunities... before we've even tried.

Big thinkers ask different questions. Instead of "Can I do this?", they ask, "What would I need to learn to make this possible?"

A manager might ask, "How can we finish this project?" A leader also asks, "How can this project improve the way our entire organization works?"

So how do you practice it? Start with a bigger goal. Identify the skills you're missing. Break the goal into smaller actions. And surround yourself with people who challenge your assumptions.

Thinking big doesn't guarantee success. But thinking too small can stop us from discovering what we're truly capable of. So think big about your destination... and stay disciplined about your execution.
